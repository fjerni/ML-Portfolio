The “AI Issue” Zine

A research-driven zine about how AI works, AI sycophancy, and its environmental impact. I designed it to make complex issues approachable and encourage critical thinking.

1 / 5
Context

This zine was created in response to Zach Kaiser’s Special Topics in Graphic Design course.

My interest in AI predates the current hype/hysteria surrounding it. As a child, I spent hours experimenting with early chatbots like Cleverbot, which could barely remember a conversation a few lines in before becoming lost and/ or insane, messing around with the primitive AI of Façade, and watching the well of training data of bots be poisoned by bad actors (RIP Tay.) AI has fascinated me long before it became a cultural obsession, and because of that I am often especially aware of the way people engage with and understand it.

Throughout the course, we examined AI from every angle. The controversy surrounding AI is difficult to define, as its impacts intersect with issues of geography, class, labor, and accessibility. Someone living in the Global South may experience AI very differently from a student using it in the United States. As AI tools become increasingly widespread, understanding these differing experiences becomes essential. Navigating AI’s role in society requires an empathetic, human-centered approach.

Challenge

From the beginning of the course, I knew I wanted to explore how people interact with AI and what shapes their opinions of it. With such an emotionally charged debate, I wanted to approach the subject with empathy, curiosity, and an open mind. Our assignment was to create a design response to the course materials that would encourage people to think critically about the subject.

I chose to create a zine, allowing my interpretation of AI to remain physically unchanging as technology and public attitudes continue to shift. In academic spaces, it's easy to fall into ideological bubbles, so I wanted to create something that would catch the attention of people who weren't necessarily interested in AI.

My challenge was to use design and rhetoric to get people thinking critically about AI use without immediately turning them away. This was difficult because AI use is so often treated as a moral issue. I had to consider how language, layout, and imagery might influence someone's interpretation of the subject. AI is so caught up in controversy and optics that I wanted to approach it without that baggage. My goal was to present the subject fairly, encouraging people to ask their own questions rather than telling them what to think.

Role

Creating the zine involved extensive research. Drawing from course readings and outside sources, I narrowed my focus to a few key issues within AI discourse. Katie Singer’s Substack was particularly influential, as I found her blunt, straightforward approach to communicating complex issues especially effective.

The design process was recursive. Every new point required me to reconsider whether I was communicating the information accurately and fairly. Simplifying such a complex subject came with three challenges: preserving important information, acknowledging the real ecological, economic, and social harms of AI, and, most importantly, avoiding language that might shame or belittle readers for their AI use. If readers felt judged or talked down to, they likely wouldn't engage with the zine in the first place. I wanted to make these issues understandable to someone unfamiliar with the subject without watering them down or turning people away.

Process

The decision to create a zine came from class discussions and my own experiences with the medium. One issue I've noticed with zines is that they tend to appeal to people who already read them, an audience that may also be more inclined to hold anti-AI views. I wanted to reach beyond that audience, so I used sleek, AI-inspired and editorial visuals to catch people's attention and distinguish the project from traditional zines.

Calling it the AI “Issue,” like an issue of a magazine, was another way to separate the project from the social associations of its medium while creating a more striking visual identity. The content informed the presentation: the sleek, AI inspired aesthetic was also a rhetorical choice, poking fun at the homogeneity of AI driven web design.

Conclusion

While I learned a lot about AI throughout this project, what interested me most was how people engage with it. A technology is only as powerful as the way it's used, and people's understanding of how AI works shapes how they interact with it socially, economically, and politically. As AI continues to evolve and cause discourse, presenting information about it in a clear, human-centered way is more important than ever.
